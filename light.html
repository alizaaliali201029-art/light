Flower

From Bouquetpedia, the free encyclopedia by amul

This article is about the reproductive structure of flowering plants. For other uses, see Flower (disambiguation).

Flower

A variety of flowering plants

KingdomPlantae
CladeAngiosperms

FunctionSexual reproduction
Common partsSepals, petals, stamens and carpels

A flower, sometimes known as a bloom or blossom, is the reproductive structure found in flowering plants (angiosperms). Its biological function is to facilitate reproduction, usually by providing the structures involved in pollination and fertilisation.

Flowers vary widely in size, shape, colour and arrangement. Some are adapted to attract insects, birds or other animals, while others rely on wind or water to transfer pollen. Flowers also have important roles in ecosystems, agriculture, horticulture, art and human culture.

Evolution

Flowering plants are among the most diverse groups of land plants. Their evolutionary history extends back to the Cretaceous Period. Fossil evidence and genetic research help scientists investigate how flowers developed and diversified over geological time.

The evolution of flowers is associated with changes in plant reproduction and interactions with pollinators. These adaptations helped flowering plants occupy a wide range of habitats.

Morphology

A typical flower consists of several structures arranged in concentric whorls. Not every flower has all of these parts.

  • Sepals: structures that commonly protect the developing bud.
  • Petals: often colourful structures that can attract pollinators.
  • Stamens: the male reproductive organs, generally consisting of anthers and filaments.
  • Carpels: structures containing the ovules; they may form a pistil.

Pollination

Pollination is the transfer of pollen from an anther to a receptive stigma. It may occur within the same flower, between flowers on the same plant, or between different plants of the same species.

Animals such as bees, butterflies, moths and birds pollinate many species. Other plants rely on wind, and some aquatic plants use water to transfer pollen.

Classification

Flowers occur across the angiosperms, a major group of plants characterised by seeds enclosed within fruits. Flowering plants include trees, shrubs, herbs, grasses and many climbing plants.

Botanists identify and classify flowering plants using characteristics such as floral structure, leaves, fruit, seeds and genetic evidence.

Cultural significance

Flowers are used in ceremonies, gardens, celebrations, decoration, literature and visual art. Their meanings differ between cultures and historical periods. Roses, for example, are often associated with affection, while sunflowers are commonly linked with sunlight.

Examples of flowers

The following examples illustrate the diversity of flowering plants.

Rose

Rosa is a genus of woody perennial flowering plants in the family Rosaceae.

Sunflower

Helianthus annuus is an annual plant native to North America.

Tulip

Tulipa is a genus of bulbous plants that often bloom in spring.

Hibiscus

Hibiscus is a large genus of flowering plants in the mallow family (Malvaceae), native to tropical and subtropical regions.

Lotus

lotus most commonly refers to the sacred aquatic plant (Nelumbo nucifera)

Orchid

Orchids belong to Orchidaceae, one of the largest and most diverse families of flowering plants, featuring colorful, specialized blooms found on every continent except Antarctica.

Peony

The peony or paeony is any flowering plant in the genus Paeonia, the only genus in the family Paeoniaceae. Peonies are native to Asia, Europe, and Western North America.

Birds of paradise

The birds-of-paradise are members of the family Paradisaeidae of the order Passeriformes. They are found mainly in New Guinea, as well as eastern Australia and the Maluku Islands. The family has 44 species in 17 genera.

Titan arum

Amorphophallus titanum, the titan arum, is a flowering plant in the family Araceae. It has a large unbranched inflorescence, a tall single leaf branched like a tree, and a heavy tuber which enables the plant to produce the inflorescence. A. titanum is endemic to rainforests on the Indonesian island of Sumatra.
